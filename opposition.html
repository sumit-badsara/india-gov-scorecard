Opposition & Protest

A democracy needs a government that tolerates dissent and an opposition that holds it to account honestly. This page checks both: which charges each party raised in opposition and whether courts or auditors later backed them, where parties changed their stand once in power, what investigations found about foreign money, and how governments of every party have handled protests.

Conduct in Parliament

When the BJP was in opposition

Whole sessions were lost: Winter 2010 (Lok Sabha worked 5% of its time) over the demand for a joint committee on 2G, Winter 2013 (8%) and Budget 2014 (21%). The 15th Lok Sabha (2009–14) was one of the most disrupted.

When Congress is in opposition

Winter 2016 (15%) over demonetisation, Monsoon 2025 (29%) and Monsoon 2026 (15%). In 2023 the ruling BJP itself stalled the Budget session demanding an apology from Rahul Gandhi.

Opposition's share of the blame

  • Both parties in opposition have treated shutting down Parliament as a tactic, which wastes the time they need to question ministers.

Government's share of the blame

  • Committee scrutiny of bills fell from 71% to 16% after 2014, record suspensions removed most of the opposition in 2023, and many bills now pass in minutes.

Patterns across parties

  • Police firing deaths have happened under Congress, BJP, CPI(M), AIADMK and coalition governments alike.
  • The largest mass detention was the Emergency (Congress, 1975–77); the largest recent ones were in Kashmir (2016 and 2019).

What changed after 2014

  • Long pre-trial detention under the anti-terror law UAPA for protest-linked cases: Bhima Koregaon accused waited years without trial, and Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam have been jailed over six years untried. UPA governments had toughened UAPA in 2008 and 2012.
  • CIVICUS, an international civil-society monitor, cut India from "obstructed" to "repressed" in 2019; it has no rating for the UPA years.

Protests that won

  • Under the UPA: Anna Hazare (Lokpal Act), Nirbhaya (tougher rape law), Telangana (statehood).
  • Under the NDA: farm laws repealed, 274 recovery notices to anti-CAA protesters withdrawn, Ladakh leader's detention revoked for talks.

So which party is the better opposition?

BJP in opposition (2004–14)

Its biggest charges, on coal and 2G allocation, were largely borne out by the Supreme Court, though the 2G trial ended in acquittals. It also shut down Parliament repeatedly and opposed policies (GST, Aadhaar, nuclear deal) it later adopted.

Congress in opposition (2014–)

Its challenge to electoral bonds was upheld and its warnings on demonetisation match the data, but its Rafale, Adani and vote-theft charges have not been upheld by courts or regulators. It too has stalled sessions and fought reforms it once proposed.

Neither party has a clean record. Each has been right on some big issues and has also used obstruction and reversals for political advantage. The table above lets you judge the specific charges for yourself.
